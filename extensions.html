Near Misses — Failed One Key Criterion
uBlock Origin (10M+ users)
MV3 deliberately limited webRequest API. Full-featured content blocker is not technically possible in MV3. Also near-zero monetization in ad blocking's open-source culture.
I Don't Care About Cookies (1M+ users)
Community fork "I Still Don't Care About Cookies" quickly became the dominant replacement. Market consolidated around one clear winner.
Stylish (2M+ users, SimilarWeb spyware)
Stylus became the universally recommended replacement. Well-established with active development.
SpeakIt! (1.4M users, DataSpii removal)
Speechify ($17.6M/year revenue, VC-funded) dominates. Read Aloud and NaturalReader also serve well. ElevenReader entering with AI voices.
Session Manager (200-500K users)
Session Buddy, Tab Session Manager, and Workona all provide robust session management. Space is well-served.
OneTab (millions, stagnating)
Still active (updated Dec 2025). Vulnerable to disruption but not orphaned — fails criterion #1.

Key Takeaway

The most reliable signal isn't user count — it's the ratio of displaced users to replacement quality. Cookie AutoDelete has 500K orphaned users and no established MV3 replacement. RescueTime has a completely empty competitive landscape. Toby's users are in active revolt with no destination.

The MV2 sunset was a one-time event — its casualties are fully identified. Going forward, the most fertile hunting ground shifts to aggressive monetization revolts (Toby, Keepa patterns) and trust-destroying acquisitions.

Productivity and workflow tools beat privacy utilities for monetization. Time trackers and tab organizers achieve 3-5% conversion at $3-12/month. Privacy tools convert under 1%. The ideal extension serves a professional workflow need with an obvious free-to-paid quality gradient.
